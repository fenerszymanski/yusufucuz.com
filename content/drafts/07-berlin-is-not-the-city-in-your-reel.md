# Berlin is ugly, and that is the point

I hear it on the first morning, usually said apologetically, usually by someone who feels a bit guilty for thinking it.

*I expected it to be prettier.*

They are not wrong, and I never argue. Berlin is flat, grey for a large part of the year, permanently under construction, and full of buildings nobody would photograph on purpose. If you have just come from Prague or Vienna, the contrast is brutal. Those cities impress you in the first hour. Berlin does not try.

What I do argue with is the conclusion people draw from it, which is that they should therefore see more of it, faster.

## The three-day problem

Almost everyone arrives with a list. It is roughly the same list, and it is long. Twelve or fifteen items across three days, most of them a train ride apart, all of them famous.

The list is not a plan. It is a form of anxiety. And the moment the city fails to be beautiful, the list gets worse, because if the walking between things is not rewarding, the only thing left is arriving at things. So people speed up. They photograph the front of a building and move on, and by the third day they have seen everything and understood nothing, and Berlin has been exactly as underwhelming as they feared.

I have watched this happen to intelligent people who read three books before they came.

## Why the ugliness is the actual material

Here is the part I would want you to take away.

Cities that photograph beautifully mostly photograph beautifully because they were spared. Berlin looks the way it does because of what happened to it, and the things that make it unremarkable to the eye are the evidence.

That empty lot in the middle of the centre is empty for a reason. That row of buildings does not match because there is a gap in it where something was bombed and what replaced it was built fast, cheap, and to a different idea of what a building is for. That absurdly wide road exists because someone was planning a capital city that was never finished. That plain concrete block is plain because it was put up by a state that had decided ornament was a lie told by the rich.

The whole twentieth century is legible in the streetscape, in order, if you are standing still long enough to read it.

That is not a consolation prize for a city that failed to be attractive. It is a better experience than being impressed by a facade, and it is why I do this work here rather than somewhere prettier.

## What I would actually do with three days

Fewer things. This is the entire advice.

Pick one memorial and do it properly, all the way to the end of the last panel, rather than three at walking pace. Stand in one square for twenty minutes and work out why the buildings around it disagree with each other. Walk one route on foot that you were going to do by train, and pay attention to the four unremarkable streets in between, because those streets are where the argument actually is.

You will "see" less. You will come home with something.

And lower the aesthetic expectation deliberately in the first hour, before the disappointment has a chance to organise your whole trip. Berlin is not going to seduce you. It is going to explain itself, slowly, and only to people who stopped moving.

## The honest caveat

None of this means the beautiful version is a lie. Summer here is genuinely extraordinary, the light on the water in the evening deserves every photograph it gets, and there are nights in this city that look exactly like the footage that brought you here.

Just do not build three days around finding four seconds of somebody else's.
