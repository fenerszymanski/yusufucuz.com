# Skip the tower: three better ways to spend the same two hours

I do not enjoy telling people not to go somewhere. Most "overrated" lists are written by someone performing sophistication, and visitors have every right to see the famous thing they came to see.

But there is one exception I make often enough that I should explain it properly, and three places I send people instead.

## The one I would skip

**The viewing platform at the Fernsehturm.**

You queue, you pay a real amount of money, you go up, and then you look out over Berlin from inside the single most interesting object in the view. That is the whole problem in one sentence.

Berlin is flat and mostly low. From two hundred metres it reads as a wide grey-green carpet with a few landmarks poking through it, and unless you already know what you are looking at, it is difficult to make it mean anything. The skyline that would justify the ticket is not there, because this city built outwards and twice over rather than upwards.

The tower itself, meanwhile, is genuinely one of the best things in the centre, and it is completely free to look at. It was built by the GDR as both broadcasting infrastructure and a highly visible symbol of the capital. In bright sunshine, the reflection on the sphere can form a cross, an effect Berliners gave their own names and stories. The object and the city around it are more interesting to me than the view from inside it.

So: look at the tower. I would not pay to stand inside it.

## Three things I would do with those hours instead

### The Topography of Terror

Free, with indoor and outdoor exhibitions, on the site where the Gestapo, SS leadership and Reich Security Main Office had their headquarters. There is also a surviving stretch of the Berlin Wall along the edge of the grounds.

It is the most serious place in central Berlin and it asks something of you. Allow an hour, more if you read properly. Nothing about it is designed to be enjoyable, which is exactly right.

### The Reichstag dome

Free and normally available with prior registration, although access can change with parliamentary business, weather and security conditions. It is worth planning for two reasons. You are in the middle of the government quarter, low enough to read the streets, and the building makes a visible argument about public access to parliament.

Check the [Bundestag's official visitor page](https://www.bundestag.de/en/visittheBundestag/dome/registration-245686) before making it part of your day; registration does not guarantee that the dome will be open.

### One museum on Museum Island, done properly

Not all five. One. My suggestion is the **Neues Museum**, and not only for what is in it. The building was left as a bombed ruin for decades and then restored in a way that refuses to pretend the damage did not happen. Shrapnel scars, missing frescoes, and new material set plainly against old. It is the clearest physical statement of how this city decided to handle its own wreckage, and you can see it while also looking at objects that are thousands of years old.

Two rooms understood beats five buildings walked through, every time.

## What I am actually arguing

Not that the tower is a scam. It is not. The argument is about arithmetic.

Most people have two or three days here. That is roughly five things done properly or twelve things done badly. The standard list is sorted by how recognisable each item is, not by how much you will understand at the end, and those two orderings are very different.

The reason I do this job is that Berlin rewards the second one. It is not a city of monuments you tick off. It is a city where an empty lot, a mismatched row of buildings, and a line of cobblestones will tell you more than any ticketed attraction, provided somebody points at them.

So spend the two hours on the ground. The tower will still be there, and it looks better from down here anyway.
