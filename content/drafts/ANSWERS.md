## Post 02 — What a tip-based tour costs me

- **No euro figures.** No per-tour amounts, no monthly income. Hard line.
- Allowed, because they describe the shape of the work rather than money:
  - Good tour: **5–10 people.** Bad tour: **1–2 people.**
  - **Almost everyone tips** (80%+) — BUT the amounts are often small enough that the tour
    does not cover its cost. Participation is high, the sum is not. This tension is the
    centre of the article, not a footnote. Do not publish "almost everyone tips" alone;
    it reads as a happy stat and misrepresents the situation.
  - Say this without euro figures: the issue is that people cannot see the two hours of
    prep, the years of reading, and the unpaid week behind a "free" tour.
  - **Convey amounts by comparison, never in euros.** e.g. two hours of work answered with
    less than the price of a coffee in the same street. No suggested tip amount either —
    Yusuf declined that. Keep the "pay what it was worth to you" framing.
  - Three causes he endorses, all of them: **the word "free"** (it sets the expectation
    before anyone arrives), **invisible labour** (nobody sees the 10+ hours a week), and
    **no known norm** (visitors from countries without a tipping culture genuinely do not
    know what is appropriate — this one is sympathetic, not a complaint).
  - Tone check: explain, do not scold. The reader is not the villain here.

## Post 08 — the reviews, read in full 2026-08-16

Stats are now **9.8/10 across 26 reviews** (was 25). Breakdown unchanged: Organisation 9.9,
Atmosphere 9.9, Content 10.0, Guide 9.9, Usefulness 9.8. Read 25 of the 26.

**Theme 1, overwhelming: "this is not the usual Berlin tour."** Eight reviewers say it
unprompted — Drenth, Al-Daftary, London, malherbe, ashley, Neuer, Dybcio, Zapendowski.
They all frame it the same way: every other Berlin tour is the Wall and the Cold War, this
one starts at the beginning. His differentiation is real and guests articulate it better
than his own marketing does. Dybcio: what surprised him was how much of old Berlin is gone.

**Theme 2: small groups are experienced as a feature.** Al-Daftary ("only 3 of us which made
it very intimate"), London, Biernat ("Small, intimate groups"), Neuer (two people, the only
ones, tour ran anyway). IMPORTANT CONSEQUENCE: what Yusuf calls a bad day in post 02 —
1–2 people — is what produces his best reviews. That contradiction is the strongest single
idea to come out of this exercise. Use it in post 02 AND post 05.

**Theme 3: he makes people see it.** Old photographs held up for now-and-then comparison,
maps, "more like a story session than a tour" (Erkan), "helped a lot with the understanding"
(Neuer), "makes you actually picture it, not just dates and facts" (Dybcio), "small details
I would have missed" (malherbe). This technique is his signature and it is not mentioned
anywhere on the website. It should be.

**The two real criticisms:**
1. London: *"It could be with more humor but no problem."* — Contradicted by Sørensen
   ("knowledgeable and funny") and Vaijyanaath ("thoughtful, funny"). Honest article material:
   the same tour reads differently to different people.
2. Fabricius: *"Yousef is not a native Berliner which reduces the background information."*
   The sharper one. It is the exact fear from post 01 (not being seen as legitimate), stated
   out loud by a guest. This is the spine of post 08 if he is willing to publish it.

Also worth noting: London mentions the blog is useful. Content already earns its keep.

**Decisions:**
- Publish the "not a native Berliner" criticism **with his answer**. It becomes the spine.
- His answer: **the reviewer is right, and reading is the compensation.** He did not grow up
  here, so he reads 10+ hours a week and refuses to say anything he cannot source. Do NOT use
  the "outsider's perspective is an advantage" defence — he did not choose it, and it would
  read as spin.
- **Quotes allowed, names removed.** Link to the FreeTour page so anyone can verify.
